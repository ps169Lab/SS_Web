The Southwest Desert Homeland

The Pueblo people live in the Southwest region of North America, in what is now New Mexico and Arizona. This environment features high desert plateaus, deep canyons, and flat-topped mountains called mesas. The climate is very hot and dry during the day, cold at night, and receives very little rain throughout the year. The Pueblo people live in the dry Southwest desert in New Mexico and Arizona. The land has flat hills called mesas and deep canyons. It is hot during the day, cold at night, and gets very little rain.

Pueblo communities—such as the Hopi, Zuni, Taos, and Acoma—have lived in this region for thousands of years. Their ancestors, the Ancestral Puebloans, built incredible villages into canyon walls and cliffs. Unlike nomadic hunters, the Pueblo people built permanent towns and stayed in one place to farm. The Pueblo people have lived in the desert for thousands of years. Their ancestors built towns into cliff walls. Unlike groups that moved to follow animals, the Pueblo people stayed in one place to farm.

Mastering Desert Farming

Because the desert gets very little rain, growing crops required expert knowledge. The Pueblo people developed clever dry farming techniques. They planted crops near the bases of mesas where rainwater naturally trickled down from rock walls after sudden rainstorms. They also built small earthen dams and irrigation canals to route river water directly into their fields. Farming in the dry desert was hard work. The Pueblo people built water ditches and small dams to guide river water to their crops. They also planted crops where rain ran off the side of high hills.

Their main crops were the "Three Sisters"—corn, beans, and squash—which grew together and protected the soil. Blue corn was especially important, used to make cornbread and porridge. Farmers also grew cotton to weave into light clothing, along with wild plants, pine nuts, and small desert game. Their main crops were corn, beans, and squash. Blue corn was very important for making food. They also grew cotton to weave into light clothes.

Multi-Story Adobe Homes

Pueblo villages were architectural marvels. People constructed multi-story apartment buildings using stone blocks and adobe, a mixture of mud, water, and straw baked hard by the hot desert sun. The thick adobe walls acted like natural insulation, keeping the rooms cool during scorching hot days and trapping warmth inside during freezing desert nights. Pueblo homes were stacked like big apartment buildings. They were built from stone and adobe mud. The thick mud walls kept the inside cool during hot days and warm during cold nights.

To protect themselves from heat and unwanted intruders, the ground floor of a pueblo often had no doors or windows. Residents used wooden ladders to climb up to the roof and lower themselves inside through openings in the ceiling. If danger approached, families simply pulled the ladders up onto the roof to stay safe! The bottom floors did not have doors on the ground.
