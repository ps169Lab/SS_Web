Different Lands, Different Resources

Long before Europeans arrived in the Americas, hundreds of Native American nations lived across North, Central, and South America. Because the Western Hemisphere is so large, it has many different environments—from icy northern tundras and windy grasslands to hot deserts and thick tropical rainforests. Long ago, hundreds of Native American nations lived across North, Central, and South America. The Americas have many different lands, like icy northlands, grassy plains, hot deserts, and rainforests.

Each environment offered a unique set of natural resources. Tribes did not choose their surroundings; instead, they had to adapt to what nature provided. Because the resources in an icy Arctic were completely different from the resources in a desert or a forest, different tribes developed completely different ways of life, homes, and tools. This is how diverse native cultures were born. Each place gave people different natural resources. Tribes had to adapt to what was around them. Because an icy land is different from a hot desert, tribes built different homes, made different tools, and lived in different ways.

Matching Life to the Land

When we study Native American history, we can see a clear rule: culture matches the environment. If a tribe lived near roaring rivers and coastal oceans, their daily life centered around fishing and building canoes. If a tribe lived on wide, flat grasslands with massive herds of roaming animals, their culture centered around hunting and tracking wildlife. When we study history, we see a clear rule: how people lived matched their land. Tribes near oceans fished and built boats. Tribes on flat grasslands hunted roaming animals.

Every invention, from underground adobe homes in the hot desert to warm fur parkas in the frozen north, was a brilliant solution to an environmental puzzle. By looking closely at the natural resources of a region, we can understand why each tribe developed its unique traditions. Every invention, like desert mud homes or northern fur coats, solved a puzzle about their land. By looking at natural resources, we can understand why each tribe lived the way they did.
